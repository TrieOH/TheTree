## Why

Payssage is the second backend to move to the ADR-0011 setup. Its one background job, webhook delivery, ran on River. `dispatchDeliveries` inserted each `webhook_deliveries` row and its River job in the same transaction (`river.InsertTx`). The worker kept its own retry count in `webhook_deliveries.attempts`, up to 5, and returned nil to stop River once it ran out.

`lib/jobs/httpjobs` enqueues with a plain POST, which can't be part of a database transaction. And on Cloudflare, the queue decides when a job runs again, not the process.

## Decision

- **The delivery row is the source of truth, and the job is a doorbell.** Dispatch commits the event's delivery rows in one transaction, then enqueues one `webhook.deliver` per row after the commit. If an enqueue fails, it's logged, not returned. We don't enqueue before the commit, because a worker could look for a row that isn't visible yet and give up on it.
- **`webhook.redrive` catches lost jobs.** Every 15 minutes (in `jobs.Periodic` and the Worker's cron), it re-enqueues pending deliveries older than 2 minutes that were never attempted.
- **The worker is safe to run twice.** A delivery that's no longer `pending` is skipped, so duplicates from the sweep or the queue do nothing. Delivery is at least once, as it was with River.
- **The queue owns retries.** A failed attempt is recorded on the row, which stays `pending`, and the job returns an error, a 5xx at `/events`. The Worker retries with backoff per message: 30 seconds, doubling, capped at an hour, up to 10 retries. The worker's own retry count is gone.
- **The dead-letter queue records the give-up.** The Worker reads `payssage-jobs-dlq` and turns each dead `webhook.deliver` into `webhook.fail`, which marks the delivery `failed` and keeps the last response. Locally, the in-process runner drops a job after its attempts and the row stays `pending`.
- **Everything else follows ADR-0011.** River, its dashboard and `SIMPLE_AUTH_*` are gone. The schema is migrated outside the process (`just payssage-goose`). Services on Cloudflare skip the deploy ledger.

## Consequences

- No separate outbox table: the deliveries table already is one. If another backend needs to enqueue inside a transaction for work with no natural row, a shared outbox in `lib/jobs` is the next step, built once there are two real cases.
- A job that waits in the queue longer than 2 minutes may be enqueued again by the sweep. Usually the second run does nothing. If both run at the same moment, the tenant gets the webhook twice.
- Locally, a delivery that runs out of attempts stays `pending`.
- Periodic schedules are written twice, as in ADR-0011.
