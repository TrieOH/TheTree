## Why

Under ADR-0011 the job semantics were split across the hop. HTTP status codes were the protocol (2xx done, 4xx drop, 5xx retry). The backoff lived in each Worker, and payssage's give-up (`webhook.deliver` to `webhook.fail`) was a table in its Worker's dead-letter consumer. The in-process runner had its own attempts and backoff and no give-up, so the same job behaved differently locally. Moving to a VPS with NATS or RabbitMQ would have meant porting that logic out of TypeScript.

## Decision

- **`Registry.Handle(ctx, Delivery) Result` owns every decision.** A carrier passes the envelope, the attempt number and whether it came off a dead-letter store. `Handle` answers `done`, `retry` after a backoff, or `drop`. The retry policy (`jobs.Policy`, `DefaultPolicy` of 10 attempts, 30s doubling to an hour) belongs to the registry. A permanent error (`jobs.Permanent`, an unknown kind, args that don't decode) drops the job.
- **Give-ups are declared at registration.** `jobs.Register(reg, work, jobs.GiveUpWith(fn))` names the job that records a give-up. `Handle` runs it after the last attempt, or when a carrier delivers the job dead. Payssage declares `webhook.deliver` to `webhook.fail` this way, and it now applies locally too.
- **Carriers only map the result.** The in-process runner waits out `After` and redelivers. `httpjobs.Handler` reads `Job-Attempt` and `Job-Dead`, and answers 200 with `{"outcome", "retry_after_seconds"}`. The Worker acks or retries with that delay and keeps a fixed 30s retry only for when the container never answered. A NATS or RabbitMQ carrier maps the same result onto ack, a delayed nak and terminate.
- **Queue `max_retries` stays above the policy's attempts,** so the queue's dead-letter only catches jobs the container never answered. Payssage's dead-letter consumer delivers them dead, so their give-up still runs.

## Consequences

- The Worker holds no job logic beyond edge work (cache purges, identityx's `jwks.changed`), which only runs inside Cloudflare.
- A Worker deployed ahead of its container still reads an old container's 204 as done.
- Periodic schedules are still written twice on Cloudflare (`jobs.Periodic` and the Worker's crons). That stays a Cloudflare-only concern: a long-lived host runs `jobs.Periodic` in-process.
