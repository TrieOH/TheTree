# ADR-0022 — A problem names its error; the mapper keeps what carries data

- **Status:** accepted (amends ADR-0016 and ADR-0019)
- **Date:** 2026-11-17
- **Scope:** `tools/go/problemgen`, `api/{identityx,informd,payssage,univents}` (`errs`, `internal/app`, `problems.yml`, `internal/features/*/spec.yml`)

## Context

ADR-0016 named the errors and put the HTTP mapping in one switch;
ADR-0019 declared each problem in YAML. A client-facing error then lived
in three places: its sentinel in `errs`, its problem in `problems.yml`,
and a case in the mapper joining them. Of about 220 cases, 198 were the
same two lines (`errors.Is(err, errs.ErrX)` → `problems.X(errs.ErrX.Error())`).
A problem declared but never mapped became a silent 500.

The details were thin, too. The sentinel's text is the problem's detail,
and most repeated the title (`invalid kid`, `invalid request`) or named
the failure without saying what to do.

## Decision

- **A problem without members names its sentinel and the sentinel's
  text** in its `x-problem`:

  ```yaml
  AddSelfToOrg:
    x-problem:
      slug: add-self-to-org
      status: 400
      title: Add Self To Org
      description: "Users can't add themselves to organizations."
      error: ErrAddSelfToOrg
      message: "you can't add yourself to an organization; ask one of its admins or its owner to add you"
  ```

  `tools/problemgen` generates the sentinel into the Backend's
  `errs/errs.gen.go` (gitignored, like the rest of its output) and
  `problems.Mapper` answers it with its problem, after the Backend's own
  switch and before the shared cases. `error` and `message` stay out of
  the bundle.
- **The message is the detail a client reads**, so it says what went wrong
  and what to do about it, in Go error-string style (lowercase, no final
  punctuation; a name like `IdentityX` may lead). `problemgen` rejects a
  message that repeats the title or breaks that style.
- **Written by hand in `errs` are only the errors a problem can't name:**
  typed errors that carry the problem's members (and the cases that pick
  a problem by what they carry), named errors answered with a shared
  problem (`Unauthenticated`, `InvalidToken`), the not-found errors and the
  check constraints. The Backend's `Problem` switch maps those.
- Shared problems (`api/problems.yml`) name no error: they answer `lib`
  errors, mapped in the generated prelude.

## Consequences

- A new client-facing error is one YAML entry. The mapper can't miss it:
  the problem and its error are the same declaration.
- `Problem(err)` alone no longer answers a declared sentinel; tests that
  check a status go through `problems.Mapper(Problem)`.
- The sentinels' doc comments moved to YAML comments beside `error`.
- The shared `lib` errors were rewritten in the same voice, so every
  detail a client reads says what to do next.
